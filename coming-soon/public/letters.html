First Words

DBW Letters

The letter that started all of this. Three run in print each month and more run online. Write to a younger self, to your mother, to your daughter, to a girl who did not make it, or to the woman you are still becoming.

Write it the way you would say it

There is no word count and no right way to do this. Write it the way you would say it out loud. The desk edits lightly for clarity and never for tone.

If you are not sure where to start, write to the woman who needs to hear it. That might be your younger self, at the age things got hard. Your mother, or the version of her you understand now that you are grown. Your daughter, or the girl you hope she becomes. A friend you lost. The woman you are still becoming.

Write about what you carried and what you put down. What nobody warned you about. The thing you finally forgave. What you wish somebody had told you at twenty-three. A win nobody clapped for. It can be four lines or four pages. It can be a poem. It does not have to be finished, polished, or happy. It only has to be true.

On the podcast: every episode of the Dear Black Woman podcast opens with a letter. Tick the box on the form and yours could be one of them.
