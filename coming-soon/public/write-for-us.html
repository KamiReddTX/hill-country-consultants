Write for DBW News

DBW News is a global publication for and about Black women. It publishes accountability journalism, cultural criticism, and reported joy, and it is looking for writers on every continent where Black women live, work, organize, grow food, make art, raise children, and hold the line.

No clips, no byline history, no pitch needed

Open to every reader

What's Going On 4 sections
  • Front and Center 1,150 words
    The biggest news story about Black women this month. You do the reporting yourself: interviews, records, named sources.
  • Word Traveled 100–150 words per item
    Short news items about Black women anywhere in the world. One tight paragraph each, with the source and the date.
  • The Record 850 words
    What lawmakers and courts did this month. New bills, appointments, rulings, trials, sentencings, and lawsuits.
  • We Are Still Asking 850 words
    Pick a story DBW already published and find out what happened next. What changed, what did not, and who still has not answered.
The Long Look 7 sections
  • The Cover 2,500–3,000 words
    The main story of the issue, built on that month's theme. Usually assigned, but pitches are read.
  • Somebody Has to Answer 1,800 words
    An investigation built on paper. Public records requests, government data, court filings, financial disclosures.
  • Say Her Name 1,150 words
    Missing and murdered Black women and girls. You must contact the agency handling the case and confirm the status on the day it publishes.
  • Across the Water 1,150 words
    One country outside the United States, covered in depth. Writers who live where they are reporting are preferred.
  • A Woman in Full 1,800 words
    A long profile of one woman. You interview her and the people around her.
  • She Was Here 850 words
    A Black woman history left out, or an older book, album, or film that deserves a second look. Sourced either way.
  • Look at Us Six to ten pages
    A photo essay. This one is for photographers. You must own or have cleared the rights to every image.
The Living 4 sections
  • Come Eat 850 words plus a recipe
    Food, written for the pleasure of eating. Cooks, restaurants, and kitchens across the diaspora. Each issue carries one tested recipe with full measurements and timing, so send the recipe with the piece.
  • Love and Everything After 850 words
    Relationships and family life. Dating, marriage, divorce, sex, friendship, motherhood, and being mothered.
  • Both Ends 850 words
    Caregiving. Women raising grandchildren, women looking after aging parents, and women doing both at the same time. What it costs, what it takes, and what the law says.
  • Pack a Bag 850 words
    Travel and time off. Where we go, where we are welcome, where we are building. Runs every other issue.
The Money 5 sections
  • Where the Money Went 850 words
    Business and economic reporting. Who is getting funded, who is getting paid, who is getting turned down at the bank, and what the wage numbers say.
  • Check the Math 250 words
    Sidebar. One statistic tracked back to where it came from. Who published it, when, how they got it, and what it leaves out.
  • Kitchen Table Money 550 words
    Personal finance. Debt, credit, buying a home, retirement, insurance, wills. Practical enough that a reader can act on it before her next paycheck.
  • She Owns It 550 words
    A Black woman business owner. What she built, what it cost her, and how the numbers actually look.
  • The Lemonade Stand 400 words
    A kid entrepreneur. What she sells, what she charges, what it cost to start, and what she has worked out about money before anybody taught her. Interview the child, not only her parents.
The Culture 5 sections

  • A Quick Word 150 words per review

What every article needs

  • A catchy title that tells readers what the piece is about and makes them want to read more.
  • A byline: the name of the writer.
  • An introduction with a hook that presents the topic and explains why it matters.
  • A main body of three to five or more well-organized paragraphs, with facts, examples, and subheadings.
  • A conclusion that wraps up the main points or gives the reader a clear call to action.
  • The right word count for the section you are writing in.
Policy

No AI-generated work

DBW News does not publish AI-generated writing: not drafted by it, not rewritten by it, not polished by it. Every submission requires a signed statement that the work is original and human-written. Submitting AI-generated work ends the working relationship, and any published piece is retracted with a public note. Spellcheck and grammar tools are fine; a generative model producing sentences, structure, headlines or research summaries is not.

The reporting has to hold

  • Sources are named. Anonymous sourcing needs prior editor approval and a documented reason.
  • Every statistic traces to its original document, not to an article that cited it. Give the author, publisher, date and page.
  • Quotes are attributed to named people, with the date and setting of the interview.
  • Allegations are attributed to charging documents and carry presumption-of-innocence language.
  • Race is not asserted unless an agency, a primary source, or the person herself states it.
  • Contradictions are printed, not resolved. If two records disagree, report both.
  • Missing-person status is confirmed with the originating agency on the day of publication.
  • Images are licensed. A direct URL is not a license: give the source, the rights status and the credit line.

What to send

Send a pitch, not a finished draft, unless you are pitching Side Eye, Written in the Stars, A Quick Word, Check the Math or Stories & Poems, which take finished work. Every pitch is read; the editors respond to the ones they want.

What gets a pitch rejected: reporting that rests on one source; numbers with no origin; personal essays sent to reporting sections; press-release rewrites; profiles arranged by a publicist with no independent interviews; stories about Black women that never quote a Black woman; anything a model wrote.

Contributor terms

Submitting does not guarantee publication: DBW receives more work than it can run, and a piece can be strong and still not fit the issue. If your work is selected you are notified, and the terms of the piece, including any fee, are agreed with you in writing before you file. On acceptance you assign your rights in the work to Dynamic Black Women Media; you keep your byline, and you may list the piece on a resume or portfolio by title, section and issue date.
